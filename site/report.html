IQ Puzzler Pro: oracle-state baseline evidence

The full-assembly baseline controller is not vision-only. It obtains object positions and orientations directly from the simulator through puzzle.observe. Images support reconstruction, recording, and comparison; they are not the runtime perception input. A separate RGB-only prototype now runs perception and single-piece closed-loop diagnostics; see visual results and the observation-boundary audit. Complete visual assembly has not been established.

The experiments below establish physical execution under privileged object-pose observations. They must not be presented as visual manipulation or general-policy successes. Arbitrary scattered starts and flat-to-pyramid disassembly/reassembly are not demonstrated.

What was verified

  1. RSS native goal: all 55 bead centers lie within 4 mm of assigned targets; pieces are still; the dynamic case rests level on the table; hands are open and clear; both wrists return home for a two-second physical hold.
  2. GaP execution: the workflow reaches its success exit and retains measured checkpoint diagnostics, failed attempts, and recovery routes. These checks share the environment grader with RSS and are not independent of it.
  3. Independent trajectory audit: a separate implementation reconstructs bead centers from recorded rigid poses, applies Hungarian matching in the case frame, estimates finite-difference velocities, and requires released stability. It shares the geometry but does not call the environment grader.

The flat independent recomputation and pyramid recomputation reject a piece displaced by 8 mm, closed hands, and the case plus pieces floating 50 mm above their recorded positions. Checkpoint worlds do not export contact forces, so checkpoint acceptance is not a contact-force proof.

None of these checks establishes a vision-only observation contract. In the current baseline, simulator ground truth still enters the controller. For a future visual policy, this information must remain solely with the evaluator.

Generalization and the requested vision-only policy

The nominal rows are convenient robot-accessible initial arrangements. The controller reacts to measured simulator poses and updates grasp offsets, but that capability is not proof of arbitrary-layout generalization.

Capability Status
Known-pose physical grasping and assembly Demonstrated in the oracle baseline
Runtime recognition from camera images alone Not implemented or validated
Reliable arbitrary scattered starts Not demonstrated
Remove a completed flat layout, then build a pyramid Not implemented or validated
Real-robot execution Not demonstrated

The visual-policy acceptance contract must separate controller observations from evaluator state. Object information must come from camera observations; robot state must come from observable sensors. Ground-truth object poses, hidden simulator contacts, and evaluator-only state must not enter the policy. The exact sensor and calibration contract must be specified before claiming vision-only execution. All baseline outcomes remain labeled separately.

Flat-to-pyramid conversion would additionally require physical extraction, temporary placement, support-aware disassembly, and regrasping before pyramid assembly. No recorded trajectory implements that complete task.

Pyramid failure exploration

The development ledger distinguishes full loose-piece episodes from preset-support diagnostics. Static targets, IK preflights, and successful preset diagnostics never count as complete task successes.

  • Early full runs disturbed seated pieces during support-arm transitions and exposed purple gripper interference.
  • The robot6 packing restricts tilted-piece normals and gives purple a graspable base-layer endpoint. Exact cover took 878,948 nodes and 99.13 seconds. All 55 lattice sites are covered exactly once by the twelve original rigid shapes. Static feasibility and reachable wrist poses remained diagnostic preconditions.
  • v15 and v21 stalled at ten seated pieces. A navy part slightly beyond 4 mm settled below threshold, but immediate regrasping disrupted it. Later logic waits and rechecks the unchanged 4 mm and 0.015 m/s criteria before recovery.
  • A local cyan test seated cyan at 1.27 mm while displacing yellow by about 32.91 mm. Later logic checks existing supports, not only the current piece.
  • Older handoff poses were unreachable. A robot6 v28 diagnostic physically transferred pink and lifted it about 35 mm after the first hand released, then failed at an unreachable target. Contact transfer was not full placement.
  • A continuous Cartesian path placed pink at about 2.63 mm with stock grippers in a preset-support test; extensions were not necessary for that improvement.
  • A red v35 trace reached about 1.89 mm, but a narrow 35 mm release trapped adjacent beads. Retreat lifted the apex again. A 90 mm release fixed this in v36, which remained a preset diagnostic, with success: false.
  • Optional contact-seated recovery opens a failed grasp, waits, and verifies the unchanged error/speed limits and existing supports after physical contact has nudged a near-seated piece into place.
  • v37 achieved the first complete stock-hardware success in 355.04 seconds, but repeated evaluation was 1/2. Its other formal run and a live take failed at cyan. The single success remains separate from v40.
  • v38 used cyan's proper rigid-shape symmetry for an outward grasp and continuous Cartesian carry, achieving about 1.47 mm locally without support damage. The same grasp with ordinary joint interpolation in v39 failed at about 11.9 mm. v40 incorporated the Cartesian motion and passed both full nominal repeats and the native live take.
  • A steeper local v41 cyan grasp failed to lift and was not promoted.

puzzle.plan_path interpolates wrist translation and rotation, solves sequential full-pose IK, and executes through motors and contacts. It has no object-pose setters and gives no general collision-free planning guarantee. The original 20 tests cover geometry, hardware preservation, seated rechecks, contact recovery, and rigid grasp symmetry.

Extended-finger diagnostics

Separate 20 mm and 40 mm extensions add 20 g and 12 collision geometries while retaining original gripper geometry and settings. New rails use friction 2.0 0.1 0.01. These are additional hardware assumptions, not video hardware; they are not merged with stock-gripper results. Original robot self-collision settings remain a model limitation.

The complete 20 mm v4 trial seated only five pieces. A v8 test with ten preset supports placed pink and dropped red, so the whole diagnostic failed. The slow extension v9 run was superseded and cancelled; exit 143 is not a scored completed manipulation failure. Final v40 uses no finger extensions.

Reproduction and provenance

The main README documents CPU setup, the actual RSS workcell, local episodes, audits, rendering, optional cloud tools, and website generation. The versioned release contains all original trajectories, RPC traces, frozen protocols, and native recordings, with a SHA-256 manifest.

The original THIRD_PARTY.lock listed upstream GaP 71549e1, but the installed RSS execution environment used its compatible fork 09e45f8. Both source trees are included as pinned submodules. RUNTIME.lock and the measured package inventory distinguish the actual installed runtime from the reference upstream checkout. Original experimental source hashes and evidence remain unchanged.

The model uses a 25 mm bead pitch and 12.5 mm radius. Mass, stiffness, and friction are monocular modeling assumptions, not measured calibration. The flat target comes from about 61 seconds in the demonstration. The pyramid uses a solver-found robot-accessible packing with different colors. No vision-only policy, photorealistic twin, robust general policy, or real-hardware execution is claimed.
